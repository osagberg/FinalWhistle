# Unthought-of gaps — brainstorm 2026-06-05

Status: BRAINSTORM — read-only ideation 2026-06-05; candidate gaps for the dynamic roadmap, owner to prioritise.

Seven-lens sweep of the codebase against the five pillars, the believability infrastructure, FM-player expectations, sim depth, world narrative, and scaling risk. Ideas already tracked in the gap-map, the feature-backlog, or DECISIONS are dropped. What remains is either genuinely new or partially-covered-but-under-specified. Grouped by theme; each carries an impact read and a near-term vs far-future horizon.

---

## 1. Pillar-readiness gaps

The hardest finding across the sweep: most pillar machinery is built and tested, but the data that would make it visible to the player is not flowing. The infrastructure is real; the experience is scaffolded. This bucket holds the per-pillar realised-vs-scaffolded verdicts plus the believability-infrastructure (Pillar-0) gate gaps.

### Procgen is a name generator, not a world generator (Pillar 1) — NEW/PARTIAL
- Impact: high. Horizon: near-term.
- `generate_team()` produces culturally-flavoured player and team names deterministically — that works. But `ContentStore.load_baked()` is a documented stub returning `Ok(Self::default())`, so player attribute generation from procgen inputs is unbuilt. No 6-tier pyramid, no procedural club generation, no per-save world descriptor. League structure, club identities, and world shape are hand-authored RON fixtures identical across every save until T4.5-B / E0 / E1 ship.
- Why it matters: "names vary" versus "worlds vary" is the entire Pillar 1 promise. A player cannot care that their striker came from a save-unique culture when the league table, rival clubs, and world structure are the same every run. Today the procedural-fantasy pillar is a name generator. Partially implied in feature-backlog §3 but never named as a Pillar-1 readiness verdict.

### Procgen player identities are name-only — no attribute diversity, no signature pre-assignment (Pillars 1, 3, 5) — PARTIAL
- Impact: high. Horizon: near-term.
- `generate_team()` produces `PlayerName` / `ManagerName` only. The 55-field attribute bundle (ADR-0002), the gene substrate (AttributeFamily PA/CA), and `signature_candidates` are not generated by procgen — they are initialised from `PlayerTemplate` fixtures via a stub loader. `build_roster_from_league` round-robins a single AM template, assigning identical gene values and signature candidates to every slot. Every player in every club in every save shares one attribute distribution, differentiated only by name.
- Why it matters: Pillars 1, 3, and 5 all require distinct starting identities. The gene→attribute compiler (T4.5-E0) is the planned fix; until it ships, "every save is a different world" is name variety. Partially covered by the "mirror substrate" references but not named as a Pillar-1 verdict.

### The ledger is fed by 5 of 30 event classes — 25 have no emitter (Pillar 2) — NEW
- Impact: high. Horizon: near-term.
- Wired emitters today: `DebutSenior`, `LegacyGoal`, `TitleWon`, `BreakthroughMoment`, `RegressiveCollapse`. The other 25 — transfers (PromisedYouthMinutes, BrokenPromise, TransferRequested/Refused, SoldUnderProtest, BoughtOnDeadlineDay, contract renewal accept/reject), relational arcs (RivalryFormed, MentorTeammate, DerbyControversy, FormerClubReunion), competition events (CupFinalWin/Loss, PromotionWon, RelegationSuffered, UnbeatenRunEnded), and career-shape (DebutClub, Retirement, InjuryLongTerm, InternationalCallUp) — have schemas but no emission path.
- Why it matters: The 30-class catalogue is the architecture of career memory, not its content. After ten seasons the ledger would hold debuts, goals, titles, and breakthroughs — no transfer stories, no contract drama, no remembered injuries, no rivalries. The readers rank correctly but have almost nothing to rank over. This is the most direct Pillar-2 gap and is only scattered across feature-backlog §3.

### Ledger→prose bridge is severed — readers emit ranked EventId lists that nothing renders (Pillar 2) — NEW
- Impact: high. Horizon: near-term.
- `PressReader::candidates()`, `FanReader::fan_callbacks()`, and `CoachReader::player_signals()` are structurally complete and tested. But the rendering bridge from a candidate event to a sentence of prose is absent from the shipped runtime. The IPC commands that use the readers render event metadata as DTOs, not prose. Tracery slot-filling is deferred (T3-3), so even events that exist in the ledger reach the user as raw event-class labels, not "the cup final you won in season 3."
- Why it matters: The ledger can only "surface decisions years later" if there is a path from EventId to readable text. Today the pipe runs match→harvest→append→candidates→DTO and stops at structured data. Distinct from the missing-classes gap: even with all 30 classes emitting, the user would see structured data, not remembered narrative. The gap between "the ledger knows" and "the player is told" is the actual Pillar-2 experience gap.

### Club-level events are not attributed to individuals — a title win enters no player's career (Pillar 2) — NEW
- Impact: medium. Horizon: near-term.
- `commands.rs` documents that only player-subject events enter the per-player ledger. Club-level events such as `TitleWon` (Beneficiary = Club) are not attributed to any individual player, accumulate no breakthrough meter, and generate no per-player callback. A striker with 10 goals in a title-winning season has no ledger trace of the title.
- Why it matters: Football careers are collective moments that belong to individuals — the title your striker won, the final your keeper saved. A `TitleWon` with no player participants is an accounting entry, not a memory. This hollows one of the most salience-worthy classes and starves the breakthrough meter of the most emotionally loaded moment in a season.

### Salience is degenerate — every event ranks by emission stakes, all stamped 1.0 (Pillar 2) — NEW
- Impact: medium. Horizon: near-term.
- `compute_salience()` returns `event.stakes` unconditionally (documented degenerate T3-2 formula). The harvester stamps every `DebutSenior`, `LegacyGoal`, and `TitleWon` with `stakes = Q32::ONE`. With uniform stakes and salience = identity(stakes), every event has identical salience; readers fall through to "most recent event of the highest-stakes type," not the most career-defining one.
- Why it matters: The readers' correctness depends on salience being a real signal. A season-1 debut and a season-8 cup final are indistinguishable, so older high-significance events decay purely by age — defeating "surfaces decisions years later." The 5-term blend that fixes this needs `participant_prominence_avg` and `rarity_boost`, which do not exist yet.

### SignatureActivated is emitted but never consumed — activated signatures stay candidates forever (Pillar 5) — NEW
- Impact: high. Horizon: near-term.
- A Kind-1 breakthrough appends `Consequence::SignatureActivated { signature_id }` to the `BreakthroughMoment` event, but no code reads that consequence to promote the signature out of `signature_candidates`. There is no `active_signatures` field on `PlayerInstance`. The signature stays a candidate; the manager cannot tell whether a move has unlocked.
- Why it matters: A direct Pillar-5 integrity failure. The whole promise rests on an activated move being distinguishable from a candidate. Commentary fires and PA lifts, but the player's card is identical to one who never had a breakthrough. Fix: add `active_signatures` to `PlayerInstance`, consume the consequence after phase 2b, remove the activated signature from candidates, surface it in the DTO.

### Signature candidate routing excludes GK, defenders, and forwards — 5 of 8 triggers can never fire (Pillar 5) — NEW
- Impact: high. Horizon: near-term.
- `role_receives_candidates` maps only midfielder slots (5-7) to candidates. GK (0), defenders (1-4), and forwards (8-10) receive none. Working predicates for commanding-claim (GK), overlapping-surge (full-back), poacher's-dart (striker), and touchline-beat (winger) can never fire via the dispatcher because those players hold an empty candidate slice. The guard is formation-slot-based, not template-based.
- Why it matters: 5 of the 8 non-stub signatures are unreachable in production matches, so the promise that different role archetypes carry different readable identities cannot manifest. Fix: author a template per RoleFamily and widen the routing guard to all slots, or derive it from content-store template coverage.

### Signature thresholds are universally 0.45 — every eligible-role player triggers on almost any tick (Pillar 5) — NEW
- Impact: medium. Horizon: near-term.
- All 8 real predicates share one threshold (~0.45) against spec values of 0.6-0.75. Comments acknowledge "T2-4 tuning restores the spec values," but the deferral has stood since T1-2b-iv and is not tracked as a concrete task. With players initialised near 0.5, virtually every midfielder fires a first-time-diagonal-switch or long-range-strike whenever the cooldown clears, regardless of whether they are a specialist.
- Why it matters: Pillar-5 identity requires selectivity — only specific players do specific things. At 0.45 the signatures are background noise, not identity markers. A single-candidate slot fires deterministically every cooldown cycle until thresholds rise to 0.6+ and differentiate per predicate.

### Biased scout archetypes are enum variants with no implementation — all scouts are neutral fog (Pillar 4) — NEW
- Impact: high. Horizon: near-term.
- `ScoutArchetypeKind` declares 7 variants but `observe_one_category` uses only a fixed noise amplitude and band half-width. The `CategoryBiases` struct exists and is validated but is never read during observation. Every `observe_player` call produces symmetric noise of identical amplitude across all three categories regardless of archetype.
- Why it matters: Pillar 4 is the strongest differentiator versus FM. With neutral fog the only variation is random noise that converges on identical reports. A physical-profiler overrating Physical alongside a technical-purist doing the opposite is the specific experience promised — and impossible until bias is applied in `observe_one_category`.

### Observation count does not narrow the band — "truth emerges over seasons" has no mechanism (Pillar 4) — NEW
- Impact: medium. Horizon: near-term.
- `UncertaintyBand` and `observation_count` exist, but `observe_one_category` always applies a fixed `BASIC_SCOUT_BAND_HALF_WIDTH = 0.12`. After 20 observations a player's band equals its band after 1. There is no formula mapping observation count to a narrower half-width.
- Why it matters: A core Pillar-4 mechanic. A manager should see a wide band on a once-scouted player and a tight one after seasons of weekly watching. Today `observation_count` is a counter with no game effect. A one-line narrowing formula in `observe_one_category` closes it.

### Only one scout is ever instantiated — multi-scout comparison cannot exist in the data model (Pillar 4) — PARTIAL
- Impact: high. Horizon: far-future (post-EA per backlog).
- `observe_match_participants` takes a single `&Scout`; `advance_week_inner` passes one `Scout::basic_uncertainty()`. `PlayerInstance.last_scout_report` holds a single `Option<ScoutReport>`, not a history or multi-scout panel. Even with archetypes implemented, the UI could not show disagreement because the struct cannot hold more than one report.
- Why it matters: The backlog flags the gap; this adds the code-grounded confirmation that the save state physically cannot hold more than one report per player. A `scout_history: Vec<ScoutReport>` or `reports_by_archetype` map is a prerequisite before multi-scout comparison is buildable.

### Narrative flags are invisible with no reveal path — LateBloomer / AwakeningDormant stay permanently hidden (Pillar 4) — NEW
- Impact: medium. Horizon: far-future.
- `CategoryBiases.narrative_flag` must be zero (enforced); flags are intentionally unobservable by scouts. But no other mechanism — coach observation, press quote, long-term ledger pattern — lets a manager discover that a player carries `AwakeningDormant` or `LateBloomer`. The flags gate Kind-2 breakthroughs the manager has no signal even exist for a given player.
- Why it matters: The scouting pillar's promise is partial opacity that erodes over time. With permanent opacity a manager can never meaningfully invest in a possible late bloomer. The game needs a partial-reveal path — a coach flag ("unusual trajectory in training data") or a memory callback ("three coaches in three clubs have noted his late pace"). A design gap, not a code bug.

### Breakthrough age-curve modifier is passed in context but never applied (Pillar 3) — NEW
- Impact: medium. Horizon: near-term.
- `BreakthroughContext.age_years` is populated (hardcoded to 22 for all players) and passed to `evaluate()`, but `evaluate()`, `accumulate()`, and `sample_positive_delta()` never read it. A 17-year-old and a 34-year-old have identical breakthrough probability profiles, and `NarrativeFlag::LateBloomer` cannot be properly gated.
- Why it matters: Age-gated development is an FM-differentiator and a Pillar-3 requirement — a late-bloomer breakthrough in year 8 should feel earned. With no age modifier, every player aged 17-37 accumulates at the same rate and no late-bloomer narrative emerges from the mechanic.

### The "co-equal required check" believability gate is wired nowhere (Pillar 0) — NEW
- Impact: high. Horizon: near-term.
- DECISIONS 2026-06-04 (believability-first, clause 3) declares the standing believable-football CI gate "a co-equal required check alongside the determinism gate." Reality: `ci.yml` runs fmt/clippy/banned-terms/determinism-audit/content-validation/`cargo test`/frontend — no `drama_sweep`. `determinism-gate.yml` runs only the bedrock-ignore guard plus hash regression. The `ci-local` recipe (`scripts/fw verify`) has no `drama_sweep`. The only invocations anywhere are the manual `just drama-sweep` / `scripts/fw drama-sweep` front doors; `fun-evaluation-harness.md` confirms "Drama CI gate (ADOPT, full, later)" is not-yet-adopted.
- Why it matters: Today nothing automatically re-checks the M1 distribution on a PR. A coefficient change or BT regression that re-bimodalises the engine would pass green CI exactly as the mean-38.85 bimodal engine passed gates T0-T4. The vision is real; the wire is missing.

### Wired today, the gate would measure the mirror substrate — the one config that provably cannot fail (Pillar 0) — NEW
- Impact: high. Horizon: near-term.
- `drama_sweep` defaults `--vary-quality false` and `--archetype-pair false`, so `differentiated` is false unless flagged, and no test or CI path passes either flag. DECISIONS 2026-06-04 notes the watchable goal-rate was tuned on MIRROR teams (both DEFAULT archetype, all players mid_range_baseline) — inputs that physically cannot exhibit the failure, exactly how a mean-38.85 bimodal engine passed four phase gates. The differentiated-roster machinery (`apply_quality_jitter`, `pick_archetype_pair`) is built and unit-tested but exercised in the suite only by `extended_seed_full_match_goal_count_realism_envelope` (one fixed archetype pair, no attribute variance, a collapse/runaway guard rather than an M1-distribution gate). The S2 "higher-rated-side-wins > chance" metric exists only as prose in `drama-model.md`: no implementation, no `S2_` field, no test.
- Why it matters: A gate that runs on mirror teams measures the one configuration that provably cannot exhibit the failure mode it is meant to catch. When wired, it must default to differentiated rosters or it inherits the blind spot DECISIONS named as the root cause of four missed gates. S2 — the metric proving transfer off the mirror substrate — is entirely unbuilt despite being a named pre-FUN-1 deliverable.

### Glitch-coherence CI gate is a named deliverable that has never been wired (Pillar 0) — NEW
- Impact: high. Horizon: near-term.
- `inspect_frames` implements all 7 DX-2 detectors (BallTeleport, BallPhasingPlayer, PhantomGoal, PersistentPlayerOverlap, ImpossiblePlayerVelocity, BallOffPitch, MatchStall) with a JSON + human report, but it always exits SUCCESS regardless of flag count, is never called from `cargo test` / CI / the ci-local recipe, and has no test asserting a report status on known-clean/dirty frames. The FUN-0 velocity fix (32,183 → 12 ImpossiblePlayerVelocity flags) was measured manually, not protected. A single-seed full-match run costs ~65ms.
- Why it matters: The 2026-06-04 believability-first decision lists a standing believability gate co-equal with the determinism gate. The determinism gate catches canonical-state drift; the glitch-coherence gate is the physical-plausibility half. Without it, a commit reverting the velocity cap, reintroducing teleportation, or breaking separation passes green CI. The drama_sweep gap is tracked separately; `inspect_frames` is the more tractable near-term wire (integer flag counts, no cross-OS float-verdict concern, a one-line exit-code fix).

### Season metrics S1-S4 are entirely absent from SweepReport — the believability gate has no season-level signal (Pillar 0) — PARTIAL
- Impact: medium. Horizon: near-term.
- drama-model.md defines four season metrics with target bands: S1 (title-race tightness, 2-8 pt gap), S2 (upset frequency, 25-40%), S3 (table-spread variance, std ≥ 6 pts), S4 (underdog over-performance, 10-25% of bottom-third finishing top half). The drama_sweep binary has zero S-prefixed fields, no season-level aggregation, no `--season` mode. The composite drama index weights S1 at 15% and S2 at 10% — that 25% is permanently zeroed in every run. Existing docs mention S2; S1, S3, and S4 are unmentioned anywhere.
- Why it matters: A gate that measures only match-level outcomes catches the 43-43 bimodal engine but not a sim producing plausible matches inside processional, title-decided-by-matchday-5 seasons. Pillar 1 is undermined by a season layer with no variance guard. S3 specifically guards against seasons that look identical regardless of seed — a failure the determinism gate cannot catch, since identical standings is not a hash collision.

### The believability oracle is structurally excludable from the cross-OS guarantee (Pillar 0) — NEW
- Impact: medium. Horizon: near-term (must precede wiring).
- `drama_sweep` and `drama.rs` are bin-local with a blanket `#[allow(clippy::float_arithmetic)]` and use f64 for mean/std/percentile and threshold comparisons (correctly exempted from the sim float ban as off-canonical tooling). The match sim feeding it is deterministic and cross-OS-pinned, so integer goal/shot/event counts are identical across platforms. But guard verdicts are float comparisons on f64 aggregates. A value on a band edge — mean 3.2000000001 vs 3.1999999999, or a p95 interpolation at the 7.0 ceiling — could flip PASS/FAIL on platform float rounding. There is no cross-OS agreement check on the drama report.
- Why it matters: The gate is meant to be co-equal with the determinism gate, but the determinism contract is what makes any gate trustworthy across the CI matrix, and a float-verdict gate inherits none of it. Before wiring, compute verdicts in integer/fixed arithmetic, pin the report, or at minimum cross-OS-diff the deterministic integer per-match metrics — otherwise the gate flakes platform-dependently, which is worse than no gate.

### The anti-scripting Goodhart guard is warn-only and reaches no gate (Pillar 0) — NEW
- Impact: medium. Horizon: near-term.
- DECISIONS 2026-06-04 clause 5 requires "an anti-scripting / unpredictability drama metric plus a scripted/predictable=fail judge dimension BEFORE FUN-1." The metric is implemented (`anti_script_p_comeback_given_trailing`, `anti_script_home_away_asymmetry`, `anti_script_suspicious` flagged when p_comeback > 0.75 or asymmetry > 0.25) and self-tested. But it is explicitly warn-only: `all_guards_pass = m1_guard_pass && m2_guard_pass && m8_guard_pass` — `anti_script_suspicious` is not in the conjunction, only prints to a human summary, and is asserted by no test. The LLM-judge half appears nowhere in code.
- Why it matters: The guard's whole purpose is to be a hard backstop against tuning the engine into a deterministic drama-faking script that satisfies rate targets. As a warn-only printout nothing reads in CI and no test asserts, it cannot fail a build — exactly the Goodhart hole it was specified to close. "BEFORE FUN-1" is the stated deadline; FUN-1 is imminent.

### Gate bands hardcode-duplicate the design doc with only a comment as the sync contract (Pillar 0) — NEW
- Impact: low. Horizon: far-future (cheap, low blast radius today).
- Guard/target constants in `main.rs` and the band comment in `drama.rs` both carry "Any change to the numbers must be made in drama-model.md first, then here" — but nothing enforces it. The numeric bands (M1_MEAN_MIN 2.3, M1_P95_MAX 7.0, M2_FIRST_THIRD_MAX 0.55, M8 9-18) are duplicated as Rust consts with the doc as prose source of truth, linked only by a human-honoured comment. There is no parse-the-doc-table parity test, despite that pattern existing for hash pins and discriminants.
- Why it matters: When the gate is wired, the bands ARE the gate. Drift between the documented contract and the enforced constants means the gate enforces something other than what the doc claims, undetectably. These bands are exactly the tuning-coefficient class that churns during balancing. A cheap doc-table-parity test would single-source the believability contract the way the determinism contract already is.

### drama_sweep exit-code contract is correct but has no regression test of its own (Pillar 0) — NEW
- Impact: low. Horizon: near-term.
- `run()` correctly returns `ExitCode::FAILURE` when `all_guards_pass` is false (covering M1, M2, M8), but no integration test spawns the binary with a known-bad input and asserts non-zero exit; the test suite checks `aggregate()` but never the `run()`→exit-code path. The contract is a single bool propagation with the same silent-failure risk the bedrock hash test had before its meta-test was added.
- Why it matters: The determinism gate has three layers protecting against silent bypass (a meta-test asserting the bedrock test isn't `#[ignore]`d, a commit hook, a CI step); the believability gate has zero layers protecting its exit-code contract. When wired, a one-line refactor (wrong variant, swapped SUCCESS/FAILURE, an early `Ok(SUCCESS)`) would silently disable it. Fix: one integration test running `run()` with a fixture engineered to fail M1 and asserting `ExitCode::FAILURE`.

## 2. Management-depth systems (FM-player expectations)

These are the manager-facing systems an FM player expects but that today exist only as mechanical skeletons or not at all. Most are FW-native because the procedural world and append-only ledger make them richer than their FM equivalents.

### Club culture and ownership personality as a persistent constraint — NEW
- Impact: high. Horizon: near-term.
- Board confidence exists as a single-axis curve. Missing: the club's inherited cultural identity as a structural constraint — a youth-built club resisting a sell-youth manager, results-first ownership demanding wins during a long-term build, a community club where big-money signings feel culturally wrong. In FM this surfaces as ownership adjectives that gate decisions independent of results.
- Why it matters: Without it, board confidence is a counter. FM's richest career tension is the philosophy-versus-ownership clash — the selling-club owner who accepts 8th but vetoes keeping your best player. FW-native: each club's culture is a procedural seed and a callback anchor ("the chairman always wanted youth-first — your record fits the brief").

### Player ambition and career-arc goals as autonomous transfer pressure — NEW
- Impact: high. Horizon: near-term.
- Transfer requests are morale-driven today. Missing: the player as an agent with career-narrative goals that generate pressure independent of morale — a happy mid-table player who "wants to compete for titles," a peaking player who "wants the highest level," a 22-year-old who "needs regular minutes." These fire as approach permissions, transfer requests, and contract refusals from happy, well-paid players.
- Why it matters: One of FM's strongest tension generators. A world-class player who loves the club but needs a title is a better story than one who leaves after two benchings. It hooks Pillar 2 directly: "you held him back" / "you gave him his title" are richer callback anchors than "he was unhappy."

### Contract clauses as a system of deferred story seeds — PARTIAL
- Impact: medium. Horizon: near-term.
- Release and sell-on clauses exist as individual backlog rows. Missing: framing them as a web of obligations that fire as future events — a buy-back clause that activates three seasons later when a sold player becomes world-class, a loyalty bonus that tips the wage bill into the red just before a window, a release clause triggered in secret with 48 hours' notice.
- Why it matters: FW's append-only ledger is built for this — each clause is a future callback. The backlog has the individual features but not the system framing: contracts as deferred story seeds that activate at fixed future points.

### Financial sustainability (FFP-equivalent) as a multi-season liability — NEW
- Impact: medium. Horizon: far-future.
- Wage-bill tracking is a per-window ceiling. Missing: the UEFA-FFP-equivalent as an accumulated liability — overspending in year 1 forces a year-3 sale crisis even when the board was quiet at the time. A league-level sustainability rule with club-specific income curves (matchday, prize money, sales).
- Why it matters: FM players plan three-season financial arcs. The current cap is a current-state check; a multi-season liability is a strategic planning layer that creates "I can afford this now but it hurts in season 3" decisions. Without it, finances are a per-window puzzle, not a career-arc constraint.

### Press conference as a strategic tool with mechanical consequences — PARTIAL
- Impact: medium. Horizon: near-term.
- Press relations exist as a narrative surface and the PressReader scaffolding is built. Missing: the decision-consequence loop — praising a rival seeds a title-race subplot, praising your striker before a derby raises his pressure-readiness, criticising a referee shifts the next match's card strictness.
- Why it matters: FM players remember the press conference that backfired. The scaffolding renders narrative but no choice has downstream effects on morale, rivalries, referee relations, or performance.

### Staff as ledger participants with their own loyalty arcs — NEW
- Impact: medium. Horizon: near-term.
- Staff exist as functional hires (coaches, scouts, medical). Missing: staff as characters who accumulate history — a six-season assistant who shows up in callbacks as "longest-serving staff member," a lower-league coach who mentored three breakthrough players, a cheap scout who proved his eye.
- Why it matters: Staff are already entities, so making them ledger participants costs little mechanically but pays off richly against Pillar 2. FM players form attachments to long-serving staff; FW under-exploits its memory ledger for non-player characters.

### Transfer deadline day as a distinct dramatic phase — PARTIAL
- Impact: medium. Horizon: far-future.
- The window models the clock and offer expiry. Missing: deadline day as a behavioural mode — compressed negotiation timelines, AI panic buys when a club loses its first choice, inflated-but-decaying valuations across a 48-hour window, and the agency inversion of a desperate late move you would never make on day 1.
- Why it matters: FM players remember deadline days specifically. The backlog has the mechanism skeleton (window state, expiry) but not the tempo-and-agency-inversion mode that makes it a moment rather than a calendar fact.

### Loan recall and loan failure as a development decision — PARTIAL
- Impact: low. Horizon: far-future.
- The loan mechanic skeleton exists (duration, obligation, recall triggers, loan watch). Missing: the recall decision-quality layer — a player at a toxic loan club regressing, and the choice to pull him (he blames you for disrupting development) or leave him (he suffers), with downstream ledger consequences.
- Why it matters: A framing addition rather than a new system: the loan-failure narrative and recall decision quality, plus its ledger trace.

### National team as a parallel career narrative — PARTIAL
- Impact: low. Horizon: far-future (multi-nation is post-EA).
- International duty is tracked as an availability flag. Missing: the national team as an off-screen force — a tournament stripping your best players for three weeks and returning them tired or injured, a stalled international career as a narrative thread ("you gave him the confidence that led to his first cap"), a national manager who doesn't rate your player creating a morale rift.
- Why it matters: Low because the backlog flags it low-leverage and multi-nation is post-EA. But the framing shift from "availability flag" to "parallel career narrative with callbacks" is uncaptured and worth doing when multi-nation lands.

## 3. Season / world simulation

This theme splits two ways: sim-depth gaps where the match engine is missing causal substrate, and world-narrative gaps where the procedural world has no history before the player arrives.

### Match context is a sealed box — home advantage, pitch, and crowd are structurally absent (sim-depth) — NEW
- Impact: high. Horizon: near-term.
- `MatchState` carries no home/away club IDs, no pitch-quality scalar, no crowd-pressure modifier. Every match runs symmetric environment for both sides, so the drama sweep cannot even measure the bias. Home advantage drives roughly 60% of contested outcomes in real football. Fix: append `home_club_id`, `pitch_quality: Q32`, and `crowd_pressure_coeff: Q32`, modulating already-wired BT-leaf utility math — no canonical-encoding churn beyond three fields.
- Why it matters: A symmetric engine cannot produce the home-win distribution real football shows. Every S2 measurement, season-arc metric, and fixture-congestion calculation is systematically wrong until environmental asymmetry is modelled; S2 ("higher-quality side wins more often") implicitly requires it.

### Match-fitness never carries forward within a season (sim-depth) — NEW
- Impact: high. Horizon: near-term.
- `play_one_match` calls `MatchState::initial_with_content` fresh for every fixture. Even once in-match fatigue (FUN-LM1) is wired, there is no mechanism to project post-match fitness back into `PlayerInstance` and forward into the next match. The carry-forward layer is structurally absent: it needs a `post_match_fitness: Q32` field, a harvest step after `tick_match`, and a pre-match projection in `initial_with_content`.
- Why it matters: Fixture congestion, rotation, and overplay injury risk are all unimplementable even after FUN-LM1 ships, because the match-to-season fitness bridge does not exist in the type system. Rotation decisions need this bridge to be a real canonical field, not a post-EA concern.

### Match-to-season feedback has no causal substrate — the MatchEvent stream is discarded (sim-depth) — NEW
- Impact: high. Horizon: near-term.
- After `play_one_match`, only `MatchOutcome { home_score, away_score }` survives; `match_events` is consumed for stats then dropped, and the tactic-FSM end state is discarded. Result: no within-season tactical-form signal, no player-form signal from actual events (a striker who missed 4 big chances has the same sharpness as one who scored a hat-trick), and no tactical memory to seed the next match. Needs a `MatchSummary` carrying per-player performance, tactic-exposure tallies, and signature-activation counts back into `PlayerInstance`.
- Why it matters: Pillars 2 and 3 both depend on match events feeding the ledger. Explicit events (goals, debuts) fire correctly, but continuous state — form, sharpness, tactical familiarity, signature-readiness drift — has no causal path. This is the architectural reason "form decay" and "cohesion ramp" are unimplementable even once designed.

### AI-fixture fast-path has no quality-differentiation model (sim-depth) — NEW
- Impact: high. Horizon: near-term.
- The hybrid policy runs the real engine for the player's club and seeded scorelines for the ~95 AI-vs-AI fixtures, but the scoreline generator is undesigned. Sampling from the same distribution as mirror-team matches yields identical goal and home-win rates regardless of the quality gap between a tier-1 contender and a tier-6 struggler. Team quality (encoded in rosters) has no path into AI outcomes, so standings are not merit-based.
- Why it matters: If 95 of 96 clubs have lottery-structured outcomes, the promotion/relegation race is noise rather than merit. A manager building a better squad should see that in the table. The fast-path needs a quality-differentiated model (a simple Poisson or Dixon-Coles parameterised by relative CA). S2 applies to AI fixtures too and has no implementation path for them.

### Signature readiness accumulates in-match but is never seeded from career state (sim-depth) — NEW
- Impact: medium. Horizon: near-term.
- The dispatcher is correct for in-match firing, and `signature_readiness` accumulates per relevant ledger event in `BreakthroughState`. But `initial_with_content` does not carry `signature_readiness` onto `PlayerState`, so every match resets readiness to zero. A 200-event veteran fires signatures as rarely as a debutant. Fix: project `BreakthroughState.signature_readiness_by_family` into match init as a starting bias on each signature's trigger threshold.
- Why it matters: Pillars 3 and 5 require signatures to feel earned and express a player's history. With identical zero-primed readiness, signatures spread uniformly across all players rather than concentrating in veterans and specialists — and the ledger tracks readiness that never reaches match behaviour.

### Player aging produces no in-match behavioural difference (sim-depth) — NEW
- Impact: medium. Horizon: far-future (depends on T4.5-E0).
- The Identity Packet encodes `peak_age` and `aging_curve`, and `progression.md` defines PA decay from age 31, but `PlayerAttributes` in `MatchState` are loaded at init and never modulated by age. A 37-year-old PA-75 and a 19-year-old PA-75 are identical to the sim. The dispatcher reads a flat `MAX_PLAYER_SPEED` for all players; aging curves have no route into `dispatch.rs`.
- Why it matters: Signing a 35-year-old for one last season, managing his minutes, watching him fade but still produce a decisive moment is a core football story. Without age modulating speed, stamina burn, and decision velocity, the veteran-versus-youth rotation decision has no mechanical tension. Prerequisite for the "retirement pressure + aging decline" backlog feature.

### Referee variation as a deterministic personality actor (sim-depth) — NEW
- Impact: medium. Horizon: far-future.
- Beyond the absent foul/card system, the deeper gap is the referee as a first-class actor with a persistent personality. No referee entity, no `referee_strictness: Q32` in `MatchState`, no pre-match assignment, no memory-ledger reputation hook. Referee variation should be a SeedLayer site modulating foul probability, card threshold, and stoppage time — all of which already affect clock and score in real games.
- Why it matters: Referee variation is one of the largest unexplained sources of match-outcome variance after team quality and home advantage. Without it, every match plays to the same implicit tolerance, removing a strategic pre-match decision (adapt tactics to the official) and a callback surface ("you've never won when this referee is in charge").

### International breaks create calendar discontinuities with no sim model (sim-depth) — NEW
- Impact: medium. Horizon: far-future.
- The season loop has a flat, uniformly spaced fixture list. Missing: a break-window concept in the scheduler, a per-player `international_duty` flag modulating pre-match fitness projection, and travel-fatigue / international-form modifiers. The same engine needing cross-match fitness carry-forward would also seed different initial fitness by calendar position — a player back from a 12,000-mile qualifier should start lower than one who rested 10 days.
- Why it matters: International breaks are a key real-world rotation pressure point — plan around them or return exhausted players to a fixture three days later. Without calendar-aware fitness seeding, the rotation decision has no cost and no reward.

### 600-tick season match budget means most matches produce zero goals, starving the ledger (sim-depth) — NEW
- Impact: medium. Horizon: near-term.
- `SEASON_MATCH_TICK_BUDGET = 600` is documented as deliberately short of FullTime (a full match is 5,400 ticks). At ~2.66 goals/5,400 ticks, a 600-tick match averages ~0.30 goals, so most season matches produce zero `LegacyGoal` events. Since `LegacyGoal` and `DebutSenior` are the only individual-player match events that enter the ledger, most matches contribute nothing beyond appearance counts.
- Why it matters: A 38-match season at this budget yields ~11 goals total across all players, distributed over 22 players per match — a single player might score 1-2 `LegacyGoal` events per season. The breakthrough meter accumulates weighted salience from ledger events; at this emission rate, meters barely move. "Careers remember" cannot be delivered when the harvest path produces near-empty ledgers. A direct consequence of the T5-5b deferral, with its memory-system impact unnoted elsewhere.

### Baked pre-career world history as synthetic ledger events (world-narrative) — NEW
- Impact: high. Horizon: near-term.
- At save-start, the bake pipeline seeds 3-10 synthetic `MemoryEvent`s per club (a relegation scare, a legendary cup run, a famous collapse, a golden generation that broke through before the player arrived) keyed to the world seed and stored with a `pre_career: true` flag. The compaction system already handles old events; readers surface them as callbacks from day one without new mechanical work.
- Why it matters: Clubs are born empty today; the ledger only accumulates events the player causes, so every save starts in a vacuum and Pillar-2 callbacks have nothing of the world's own past to fire. The pipeline already makes names, archetypes, and fixtures — adding seed-deterministic history costs a content type plus a small procgen pass and pays off on every reader callback, the core retention hook.

### Club momentum state — baked recent-form narrative before career start (world-narrative) — NEW
- Impact: high. Horizon: near-term.
- Each club gets a baked momentum state (ascending, plateau, declining, crisis) from world-gen tier history — not a simulation of pre-career seasons, but a structured artifact of 3-5 events describing the last two (a promotion, a cup run ended by a controversial loss, a squad exodus). It feeds the board intro, press context, and initial morale distribution so the first press conference fires a contextual callback, not a generic welcome.
- Why it matters: Every club feels identical at save-start: names differ but no club has a recent story, and the board intro, press question, and fan reader have no world-context to draw from. The DESIGN_DOC "first 30 seconds" promises notable archetypes are visible, but procgen has only the archetype label, not the texture of the last two seasons. Momentum state is that texture, at near-zero mechanical cost.

### Procedurally baked inter-club rivalry graph with heat and founding incident (world-narrative) — PARTIAL
- Impact: high. Horizon: near-term.
- At bake-time, each club gets 1-3 rivals from its tier and adjacent tiers, a "founding incident" string (a cup final, a controversial relegation, a cross-city poaching), and a deterministic initial heat value. `RivalryFormed` exists but only fires reactively in-career, so saves start with a world where no clubs have history. The baked graph bootstraps the `rivalry_boost` salience term from day one.
- Why it matters: The backlog's "club rivalry depth" covers in-career escalation but not the pre-baked substrate that makes rivalries feel inherited. A derby feels different when fans say "we haven't beaten them since the season the ref stole it" versus a rivalry that begins the moment you first play them.

### Club legends file — baked retired-player biographies as callbacks (world-narrative) — PARTIAL
- Impact: high. Horizon: near-term.
- Each club's procgen pass generates 1-3 legends: retired players who never appear as active entities but whose baked biographies (a golden boot in the old tier-1 era, a loyal one-club man who never won a title, a controversial departing captain) feed the fan reader, press questions, scout prose, and the board intro. Pure content RON plus Tracery slots and one reader query.
- Why it matters: The backlog's "inter-save manager legacies" covers exporting your own career; this is the complementary pre-baked substrate — legends that already exist at save-start, seeded by the world generator. Different ideas at different horizons (pre-baked versus earned). Without pre-baked legends, the fan and press readers have nothing to invoke about a club's history in the first several seasons.

### Club identity tier — founding-era archetype shaping cultural texture (world-narrative) — NEW
- Impact: medium. Horizon: near-term.
- A single enum on the club's procgen struct (Old Money, Industrial Upstart, Market Town, New Money) feeding commentary phrase banks, initial squad attribute biases (old money skews composure/technique; industrial upstart skews physical), and board-confidence arc templates.
- Why it matters: All clubs are mechanically equivalent at save-start beyond their archetype formation; the only culture artifact today is the name bank. A founding-era archetype makes one club feel like a different job from another before kickoff — the prerequisite for the player's first 30 seconds to read as a choice, not a random assignment.

### Cultural identity drift within a save (world-narrative) — NEW
- Impact: medium. Horizon: far-future.
- A read-side ledger projection (count cross-culture signings per club per window) outputs a `cultural_composition` vector for Tracery slots — no new canonical state. A club in an Anglo region signing five Elvish-coded players over three seasons gradually picks up cross-cultural press commentary, chemistry notes, and scout-prose flavour.
- Why it matters: Cultural identity is promised as a differentiator but is frozen at bake-time. Real clubs acquire cross-cultural identity through the market over decades. A 10-season save with an evolved hybrid identity is the "every save is a different world" promise extended through time, requiring only a new reader and template slot.

### World-level era events that alter tactical context across all clubs (world-narrative) — NEW
- Impact: medium. Horizon: far-future.
- At save-gen, the pipeline draws 2-4 era events preceding career start (The High-Press Decade, The Defensive Generation, The Reconstruction Era), baked as world-descriptor fields seeding archetype distribution and press-template flavour. No sim change — content-pipeline metadata.
- Why it matters: The T4.5-H world-gen descriptor is planned structurally but has no narrative spec. Era events give the world a tactical era-feel: counter-attacking in The High-Press Decade reads as maverick; the same style in The Defensive Generation is the status quo. The same archetype reads differently by world context.

### Generational talent wave — baked cohort of exceptional players (world-narrative) — NEW
- Impact: medium. Horizon: far-future.
- At save-gen, one cohort year (3-7 seasons out) is designated a talent wave: a cluster of prospects with above-average hidden ceilings across multiple academies. Scouts report unusual depth around season 2-3; the cohort breaks through league-wide by season 5-6. A seed-derived PA distribution bias baked into Identity Packets, discovered through normal scouting uncertainty.
- Why it matters: Real golden generations are league-wide waves players experience as a cluster of talent appearing at once. FW's breakthrough system fires individually with no cohort-level wave to scout, compete for, and contextualise. A save where your striker starred in the '26 wave feels distinct from one where he was an outlier in a thin generation.

### Cross-save persistent world lore (world-narrative) — PARTIAL
- Impact: medium. Horizon: far-future.
- Starting a new career in the same world seed loads prior careers' legend records as additional baked legends, cited in press callbacks and club histories ("the club hasn't won the league since the Talbot era"). The mechanism is a world-seed-keyed lore pack written at end-of-career and read at new-save generation.
- Why it matters: The backlog's "inter-save manager legacies" surfaces your own past self; this surfaces any prior career's world-impact — a CK3-style "this dynasty ruled here before you" texture, procedurally generated and unique to the world seed. Extends the same substrate from individual legacy to shared world lore.

## 4. Scaling / risk

These are not feature gaps but structural traps: tests measuring the wrong surface, O(n) paths that degrade silently over long saves, and save-format / ID-namespace decisions being locked now that are cheap to get right today and expensive to retrofit after EA.

### Full-sim perf gate measures the wrong thing — 36x gap to 96-club scale — PARTIAL
- Impact: high. Horizon: near-term.
- `full_season_perf_under_30s` is `#[ignore]`-gated and tests 380 fixtures × 600-tick proxy for a single 20-club league. The T4.5 pyramid is ~6 tiers × ~96 clubs ≈ 1,504 fixtures/season; at the full 5,400-tick budget the back-of-envelope is ~18 min per season advance — 36x over the current gate. FUN-FP (`simulate_fixture_cheap`) is the known mitigation, still TODO, and a gating dependency of T4.5-G.
- Why it matters: A 36x gap discovered on T4.5 ship day is a multi-week excavation. The gate silently tests neither the real tick budget nor the real club count, so it stays green to T4.5 and FUN-FP regressions re-enter undetected. Every month the world-sim grows wider before FUN-FP is gated, the harder the excavation.

### Ledger O(n) readers silently degrade over multi-season careers — NEW
- Impact: high. Horizon: near-term.
- `rebuild_indexes()` clears and re-scans the full events Vec on every dirty+read; `compact()` nulls tick fields but keeps events, so the ledger only grows; the salience reader falls through to a full linear scan for non-Subject roles. At 96-club scale (~7,720 events/season) a 20-season career hits ~154,400 events, making every non-Subject salience read and every dirty-flag flip an O(154k) scan. The save perf benchmark only exercises 1,000 events on V2; no graduated-scale ledger benchmark exists.
- Why it matters: Latency creep is invisible in single-season CI. Salience reads, match-event rendering, and commentary generation will degrade across seasons until users report "the game slows down in long saves" — a hard class to root-cause post-ship. Partial BTreeMap indexes, incremental rebuild, and compaction that actually removes events are 5-10x cheaper to architect now than to retrofit into a shipped save format.

### content_pack_version hardcoded to 1 — save corruption risk on every post-EA content patch — NEW
- Impact: high. Horizon: near-term.
- `commands.rs` hardcodes `content_pack_version: 1` on every save; the load path defers the mismatch guard to `mod_load_fingerprint`, which has zero implementation. Two saves from different content packs are indistinguishable at load; a patch changing procgen seeds or club IDs silently loads against a save built on the prior corpus; the mod load-order fingerprint is never stamped or verified.
- Why it matters: Post-EA content patches are guaranteed, and the first one will silently corrupt every existing save's regenerated roster-versus-ledger cross-references unless this is wired before EA. The fix — stamp and compare a BLAKE3 hash of the content corpus manifest — is trivial to add to SaveV4 now; deferring it costs a breaking schema bump plus a migration once EA saves exist in the wild.

### SaveV4 roster regeneration has no 96-club round-trip regression test — NEW
- Impact: medium. Horizon: near-term.
- SaveV4 regenerates the full roster from `career_seed` on load (Option A). Determinism at 96-club scale (2,112 players × 22 slots × 6 tiers) has no regression test; the save perf test only does 1,000-event V2 round-trips, and the canonical-hash test covers match-tick state, not save-load. A regen deterministic at 20 clubs can break at 96 via an implicit ordering assumption or a narrow-int overflow.
- Why it matters: Regen non-determinism at scale manifests as "my players changed after loading" — a catastrophic trust violation in a career game. Cheap to add now (one integration test, deterministic seed, assert roster hash); after the pyramid is wired, a regen bug found via player reports needs a save migration and a hotfix.

### PlayerId namespace has no allocation plan for newgens and transfers above base range — NEW
- Impact: medium. Horizon: near-term.
- `roster.rs` defines `ROSTER_PLAYER_ID_BASE = 1_000_000` with bijective `BASE + club_index*22 + slot` encoding, maxing at 1_002_111 for the 96-club pyramid. There is no range, registry, or scheme for players born or transferred in after save creation. Once saves with IDs up to ~1,002,111 are in the wild, any newgen scheme must start above that range, reuse vacated slots (ledger collision risk), or force a breaking migration.
- Why it matters: PlayerIds are ledger foreign keys referenced by MemoryEvents, scouting records, and commentary templates. An ad-hoc post-EA newgen scheme bolted onto a shipped ledger is a data-integrity minefield. Reserving a range (newgens start at 2_000_000) or moving to a u64/UUID scheme costs one line now and a breaking migration later.

### Name collision near-certain at 2000+ procedurally generated players — NEW
- Impact: medium. Horizon: near-term.
- Procgen generates names via a single sequential RNG with no cross-player uniqueness check; the only existing uniqueness test is a Markov chain-quality check. At ~2,112 players, birthday-paradox math needs >4M distinct combinations for a 50% non-collision chance; a 50×50 name corpus (2,500 combinations) makes collisions near-certain.
- Why it matters: "Marcus Thornton scored against Marcus Thornton" is a believability breach and a latent data-integrity risk where PlayerId is the true key but the UI surfaces name as identifier. Adding enforcement after the save format locks needs a breaking migration (store the used-names set) or a per-load O(n) rebuild; a per-culture used-name bloom filter is trivially cheap before V4 is widespread.

### Canonical hash test covers mirror teams only — quality-gradient invariants vanish on rebaseline — PARTIAL
- Impact: medium. Horizon: near-term (the T4.5 rebaseline is the natural window).
- The BLAKE3 regression pins a fixed-seed match between same-quality mirror teams. When T4.5 lands differentiated rosters the hash will be repinned — but again as a single scenario. No proptest or battery asserts cross-gradient invariants: "a higher-rated side wins more often than chance over N seeds," "cross-tier goal counts stay in band," "a 96-club season produces no negative-goal totals."
- Why it matters: The T4.5 rebaseline is the last natural moment to broaden the regression anchor to cross-tier fixtures before differentiated play is the default. After rebaseline, a BT coefficient change that converges all matches to 3-3 regardless of quality passes the hash gate and is caught only by a human re-running drama_sweep.

### CLUBS_PER_LEAGUE fixed-size array makes the T4.5-B pyramid refactor structurally wide — PARTIAL
- Impact: medium. Horizon: near-term.
- `league.rs` defines `CLUBS_PER_LEAGUE: usize = 20` and `generate_fixtures` takes `&[ClubId; CLUBS_PER_LEAGUE]` — a fixed-size array, not a slice. The T4.5-B pyramid needs tiers of differing sizes; changing the signature to `&[ClubId]` touches every call site and propagates into fixture generation and schedule building, with canonical-hash implications because the schedule is deterministic state.
- Why it matters: Not an architectural blocker but a blast-radius amplifier: a one-row task becomes a multi-crate refactor with a hash repin. Every week the fixed-size API accretes call sites, the blast radius grows. Switching to a slice now, while the call graph is shallow, is a one-afternoon task.

*Pillar-readiness verdict: Pillar 2 (careers remember) and Pillar 4 (scouting uncertainty) are the most scaffolded — their readers, ledger, and uncertainty bands are built and tested but fed by almost no data (5 of 30 event classes, neutral-fog scouts, degenerate salience, no prose bridge). Pillar 5 (signature identity) is half-wired — the dispatcher works but activation is never consumed and 5 of 8 triggers can't reach the players who need them. Pillar 1 (procedural world) is realised at the name level only. Pillar 3 (breakthrough development) has the mechanic but no age-curve or match-feedback substrate. Pillar 0's believability gate exists as a runnable tool wired into nothing. The match-to-season causal layer (fitness carry-forward, MatchSummary, environmental context, AI-fixture quality model) is the single highest-leverage architectural prerequisite — multiple pillar gaps trace back to it.*
